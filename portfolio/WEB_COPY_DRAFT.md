## Architecture

### Folded Courtyard

**Subtitle**  
A Barcelona corner apartment shaped by courtyards, grilles, and directed views

**Metadata**

- Year: 2023
- Location: Les Corts, Barcelona
- Type: Individual work
- Category: Architecture
- Theme: Warm ivory

**Short summary**

Folded Courtyard reinterprets Barcelona's corner block through a vertical communal courtyard and a layered grille facade. Together, these two systems guide movement, frame visual encounters, and negotiate the relationship between private dwellings and public life.

**Overview**

The project is located on a street corner in Les Corts, Barcelona. It grows from a study of Barcelona's urban development, close observation of buildings surrounding the site, and the changing social role of the European balcony.

The folded courtyard acts simultaneously as an internal public room, a vertical circulation system, and a visual connector. It separates the public corner at ground level from the residential spaces above while linking different floors through carefully positioned openings. Perforated aluminum surfaces reflect the sky and surrounding buildings, extending the perceived depth of the interior.

Across the facade, three superimposed grille patterns create gradients of density and color. The system filters views and light while allowing the building to shift in appearance as the observer moves around the corner.

### Island for the Stateless

**Original portfolio title**  
Island for Stateless

**Subtitle**  
An industry-oriented land-art system for a community without land

**Metadata**

- Year: 2022
- Location: Tonle Sap Lake, Cambodia
- Type: Individual work
- Category: Architecture
- Theme: Warm ivory

**Short summary**

Island for the Stateless proposes a productive artificial landscape for stateless communities living on Tonle Sap Lake. Camouflage, infrastructure, aquaculture, and changing water levels form an island that supports life before gradually returning to landscape.

**Overview**

Tonle Sap Lake is home to stateless communities of Vietnamese origin, Cham people, and Cambodians who have lived for generations in floating villages. Without legal access to land, many residents depend on fishing and rice cultivation while remaining excluded from the territorial systems surrounding them.

The project questions the Westphalian alignment of political borders with geographic territory. Such boundaries define land but often fail to describe the lived positions, beliefs, and suffering of people whose homes do not fit within those lines.

In response, the proposal creates an industry-oriented artificial island. Breeding ponds, cooling pools, open water, productive ground, and concealed infrastructure support a temporary collective life. A camouflage-derived surface allows the landform to appear and disappear across scales and seasons.

The project is also designed to outlive its initial program. If territorial conflicts are resolved and stateless residents are able to return to land, vegetation gradually overtakes the island. Over 120 years, an inhabited infrastructure becomes a landscape.

**Section headings**

1. A Home Without Land
2. The Limits of the Border
3. Tonle Sap and the Floating Villages
4. Camouflage as Territory
5. Productive Water
6. Island Infrastructure
7. Seasonal Transformation
8. Post-Anthropocene
9. From Settlement to Landscape

### Infinitas Hotel

**Subtitle**  
A reflective urban hotel organized around a continuous public courtyard

**Metadata**

- Year: 2024
- Location: Nanjing, China
- Type: Individual work
- Category: Architecture
- Theme: Black

**Short summary**

Infinitas Hotel is a mixed-use hotel, entertainment, and conference complex whose black reflective facade changes with the sun. A sequence of diagonal bridges connects public programs through a continuous courtyard.

**Overview**

Modern glass architecture once promised transparency, but decades of uniform curtain-wall construction have produced increasingly anonymous cityscapes. Infinitas Hotel reinterprets the facade as an active urban surface rather than a neutral envelope.

Black reflective glass shifts in color according to sunlight and viewing angle. The facade maintains continuity across the site while protecting the privacy of hotel guests. Its layered geometry gives the building a distinct silhouette without relying on applied ornament.

Inside, bridge-like connections span diagonally between the conference center and lobby. These crossings tie public programs into a continuous courtyard and establish visual relationships across multiple levels.

The building combines hotel rooms, conference facilities, restaurants, wellness spaces, entertainment, and commercial programs within a coordinated structural and circulation system.

### Countryside Introduction Planning

**Subtitle**  
A carnival of anti-gentrification for folding city and countryside together

**Metadata**

- Year: 2022
- Location: Japan
- Type: Group work
- Category: Architecture / Urbanism
- Theme: Warm ivory

**Short summary**

Countryside Introduction Planning imagines rural culture, production, and landscape entering the city as an active competitor rather than a nostalgic image. A dynamic land-exchange system replaces empty urban spaces with rural programs.

**Overview**

The project begins with the idea that the countryside and the city should no longer be treated as separate conditions. Their merger becomes a form of rural urbanism and a possible response to the future folding of urban and rural life.

The struggle against gentrification is represented as a carnival inspired by Japanese parades. Rural symbols and programs move into vacant urban buildings like a procession, replacing or transforming existing spaces.

In this exchange, the countryside is no longer only a producer serving the city. It becomes a cultural and commercial competitor. The city, in turn, gains a more specific identity through the introduction of rural landscapes, production systems, and traditions.

A classification system for rural types is connected to a monitoring system for urban space. Together, they determine where exchanges can occur and how rural programs can enter the metropolitan fabric.

### Trojan Forest

**Subtitle**  
An urban forest and wetland that reveals, conceals, and repairs

**Metadata**

- Year: 2025
- Location: Charlestown, Boston
- Studio: Harvard GSD Core IV
- Instructor: Lorena Bello Gomez
- Type: Individual work
- Category: Landscape
- Theme: Warm ivory

**Short summary**

Trojan Forest is organized around decisions of visibility: what should be seen, what remains hidden, and what quietly transforms over time. Forest and wetland form a veil between Charlestown and the infrastructure of I-93.

**Overview**

Situated in Charlestown, Boston, Trojan Forest combines an urban forest with a constructed wetland. The landscape functions both as a veil and as a buffer, mitigating the visual and environmental intrusion of the I-93 highway.

The forest softens the transition between Charlestown and Cambridge, introduces shade, and expands public access. Beneath and alongside the highway, wetlands filter polluted water and support soil remediation.

The intervention unfolds through multiple phases. Each phase addresses a different environmental or social condition, from contaminated ground and flood risk to habitat and public occupation. Rather than presenting one final image, Trojan Forest is conceived as an adaptive process responding to the site's continuous transformation.

### S + O + S

**Expanded title**  
Sea + Oyster + Shoreline

**Subtitle**  
An aggregate industry for seafood waste in shoreline restoration

**Metadata**

- Year: 2024
- Location: Boston, Massachusetts
- Studio: Harvard GSD Core III
- Instructor: Adam Anderson
- Type: Individual work
- Category: Landscape
- Theme: Black

**Short summary**

S + O + S turns discarded oyster shells into a shoreline-protection system. Oyster farming, restaurants, seasonal labor, aggregate production, and wave attenuation form a circular coastal metabolism.

**Overview**

Millions of oysters are consumed worldwide each year, leaving tons of shells as waste. Boston produces oyster-shell waste while also facing increasing coastal flood risk. The project treats these conditions as parts of the same material system.

Discarded shells are collected and processed into an oyster-shell concrete used to construct wave-attenuating elements for the Boston Harbor Islands. Rumney Marsh Landfill, located beyond the most immediate flood exposure, becomes the industrial stage for drying, crushing, mixing, casting, and distributing the material.

Oyster farms and restaurants supply the shells. Seasonal workers move between aquaculture, production, and shoreline stewardship, connecting the biological cycle of oyster growth with a parallel cycle of human labor.

The result is not a single protective object but a distributed metabolism linking sea, oyster, industry, labor, and shoreline.

## Installation

### The Invisible Sisyphus

**Subtitle**  
A kinetic installation about repetitive labor and invisible technological control

**Metadata**

- Year: 2024
- Type: Individual project and thesis; group research
- Exhibition development: Kirkland Gallery, November 2024
- Category: Installation
- Theme: Black

**Short summary**

The Invisible Sisyphus is a kinetic satire of factory oppression and repetitive labor. Body fragments, machine symbols, and programmed motion create an object that cannot be identified as entirely human or entirely machine.

**Overview**

In Greek mythology, Sisyphus is condemned to roll a stone uphill for eternity. The project connects this punishment to factory workers who repeat the same actions every day while the public's attention remains fixed on the products they manufacture.

The installation responds to the ways modern technology can control working bodies. Drawing from Langdon Winner's writing on the politics of artifacts, it examines how workers become held hostage by machines and gradually become part of the machine itself.

Patterns derived from ribs, the cervical spine, pelvis, humerus, and technological components are repeated across the installation. Their superimposition makes the object difficult to classify as human or mechanical.

An Arduino-controlled motor moves the installation through a continuous cycle. Repetition is not simply represented on its surface; it is embedded in the behavior of the object.

### The Cloud

**Subtitle**  
A spatial interaction about illness, death, and the fragile rhythm of breath

**Metadata**

- Year: 2025
- Type: Individual project
- Context: Harvard Conflux
- Category: Installation
- Theme: Black

**Short summary**

The Cloud is a sensor-based spatial installation that explores how people confront illness and death. Airflow and changing light make a suspended cloud appear to breathe in response to the audience.

**Overview**

The project grows from personal experience and asks how people might confront illness and death through a shared spatial encounter.

A cloud occupies the center of the installation as an image of disease and mortality. Controlled airflow gives the form subtle movement, while changes in light make it appear to breathe weakly.

Sensors connect the cloud to the audience. As visitors approach and move through the installation, the cloud, light, sound, and atmosphere respond. The work does not attempt to resolve the fear of death; it creates a temporary environment in which that fear can be perceived and considered.

The interaction develops from image to space through layered cues involving sound, scent, food, vapor, and fish. When the stages are superimposed, they produce a time-lapse-like record of movement and changing spatial conditions.

### Anti-Wastecolonialism

**Subtitle**  
A bionic installation made from domestic waste

**Metadata**

- Year: 2023
- Type: Individual work
- Proposed context: Harvard Art Museums Art Festival
- Category: Installation
- Theme: Black

**Short summary**

Anti-Wastecolonialism transforms discarded domestic material into a group of light-responsive, bionic installations. The work links the unequal geography of exported waste to a public act of collection and making.

**Overview**

Developed countries export large quantities of waste to developing and underdeveloped regions in order to improve domestic recycling figures. This movement of material hides serious failures in waste-management systems and transfers environmental harm to communities living near sources of plastic pollution.

The project uses domestic waste as the material for an artwork that asks viewers to reconsider where waste goes and who is made to live with it.

Three to four bionic installations are designed to occupy the lawn of Harvard Yard. Each is made entirely from collected trash. Before the exhibition, public collection events gather bottles, packaging, and other discarded material from campus.

Infrared sensors and solar-powered lights allow the objects to respond when pedestrians approach at night. The installations appear to creep through the grass, turning waste into a visible and unsettling public presence.

### Three Body

**Subtitle**  
An interactive textile environment that turns social forces into changing orbits

**Metadata**

- Location: Konstnärshuset, Stockholm
- Exhibition context: Harvard Conflux / Three Body
- Type: Collaborative interactive installation
- Year: 2025
- Exhibition dates: March 20 – April 12, 2025
- Role: Design team
- Category: Installation
- Theme: Black

**Short summary**

Three Body uses the unresolved scientific Three-Body Problem as a metaphor for social relationships. Visitor reflections are translated into a changing field of fabric, sound, and projected planetary trajectories.

**Overview**

Presented by Harvard Conflux at Stora Galleriet in Konstnärshuset from March 20 to April 12, 2025, Three Body is a kinetic spatial and textile installation developed by a collaborative team spanning design, software, machine learning, and physical integration. Frieda Fan contributed as a member of the design team.

The work is inspired by the unresolved scientific phenomenon known as the Three-Body Problem. It does not attempt to explain the science. Instead, science acts as a mirror for an everyday social condition: the relationship between two people is always affected by other connected bodies, including those not physically present or consciously considered.

Visitors answer the question “Who changes your orbit?” Sentiment analysis and a physics-based system translate those responses into projected planetary trajectories, ambient sound, and an evolving social network beneath a suspended textile enclosure.

3. Bodies Not Present
4. Who Changes Your Orbit?
5. A Textile Enclosure
6. Sentiment into Force
